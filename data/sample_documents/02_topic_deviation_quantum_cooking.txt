Quantum computing leverages the fundamental principles of quantum mechanics to perform computational calculations exponentially faster than classical Turing architectures.
Unlike classical bits which remain constrained to binary states of zero or one, quantum bits or qubits exploit superposition to exist simultaneously in combinations of both eigenstates.
Quantum entanglement creates non-local correlations between distinct qubits, establishing computational state spaces of immense dimensional complexity.

Decoherence represents the primary engineering obstacle in scalable quantum processor design, where thermal noise disrupts fragile quantum wavefunctions.
Advanced quantum error correction codes distribute logical information across manifolds of physical qubits to preserve coherence during complex gate operations.
